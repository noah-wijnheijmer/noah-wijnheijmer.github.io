01 — Track record

Where the work happened

Four countries, countless projects, one method: take a system apart, find where it actually loses, and be honest about what I find. Click any role to open it up.

  • Built a three-node quantum federated learning pipeline end to end. local training, compression via amplitude encoding of 32-parameter updates onto 5 qubits
  • Compared three sign-recovery strategies under realistic shot noise: a classical side-channel, an interference readout using an ancilla qubit, and simply discarding sign. Each has a different, quantifiable price.
  • Derived and validated the closed-form cost model: relative error ≈ 2.83/√shots, confirmed against real measurements to within 4%.
  • Showed the gap widens with target accuracy. Each extra classical bit roughly halves the error; each extra quantum shot buys only a square root's worth. At 1% error, the quantum route costs over 30× more.
  • Tested the proposed coherent aggregation fix live. At the real updates' measured 0.72 mean pairwise alignment it saved about 20% of shots — real, but modest, and it doesn't price the cost of physically combining the states.
  • Published the findings as an interactive companion that recomputes every figure in the browser from the project's own data, rather than shipping static plots.

03 — Featured project

Privacy-preserving quantum federated learning

Infosys, Bangalore, India 2026.

Infosys Limited · 2026

Can you make federated updates cheaper by putting them on qubits?

Three federated nodes train a small quantum classifier and have to share an update every round. An update is 32 real numbers, and 5 qubits can hold exactly 32 amplitudes. So the idea is to send 1 quantum object instead of 32 numbers.

Loading them on is the easy half. Reading them back out is where it falls apart: measurement returns probabilities, so sign is lost, and every shot is a fresh copy that has to be sent. Count that honestly and the compression disappears.

The interesting part isn't that it fails. It's that it fails predictably, at a rate I can prove.
